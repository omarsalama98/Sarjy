## Why it's written this way

- **Concrete asks first.** The GitHub username and the keys are things only they can give you. Those are the reason to send an email at all.
- **The direction is stated, not asked.** "Does the domain factor in?" invites a flag without making you dependent on the answer. If they say nothing, you've still told them what you're doing — which is itself a communication-rubric tick.
- **The last line is deliberate.** "I'm not waiting on a reply to get going" tells a ten-person team you're self-starting over their weekend rather than idling. That reads well and costs one sentence.
- **Short on purpose.** They're ten people and this is their weekend.

## Follow-up updates to send later

The rubric grades communication separately, so plan these:

- **After the first deploy** — the URL, so they can poke at it before the presentation.
- **Once the deep dive has a result** — one number and one thing that didn't work.
- **Before the presentation** — the Loom or PDF.
- **Any day with no progress** — the brief explicitly invites "busy day, no time today." Silence is the only wrong answer.
